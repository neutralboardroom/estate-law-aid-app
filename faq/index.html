Estate Law Aid FAQ

Plain-language answers for wills, trusts, powers of attorney, health care directives, document review intake, probate, and estate administration.

Can I create a will through this platform?

You can create a will intake and starting summary. This workspace does not claim to create a valid signed will by itself.

Can I update an existing will?

Yes. You can describe what changed and what you want reviewed or updated.

Can I upload my current will?

This workspace can track document names/descriptions only. Use copies only and follow the secure-upload instructions when upload is available. Do not send originals.

Can I create a trust?

You can start trust planning intake and gather trustee, beneficiary, asset, and funding questions.

Can I update or restate an existing trust?

You can describe the trust, amendments, restatement needs, and changes to discuss with an attorney.

What type of trust do I need?

The tool can organize facts and questions, but it does not tell you a trust is definitely needed or select a final trust type for you.

Do I need a will, trust, or both?

The starting point helps organize what may be worth discussing. Final advice should come from a licensed attorney.

What is a power of attorney?

It is a document that may allow someone to act for financial or legal matters. Requirements vary by state.

What is a health care proxy or advance directive?

It is a document that may name someone to make health care decisions and record health care wishes. Requirements vary by state.

Can I help a parent with estate planning?

You can start a family-helper intake. Helping someone else may require authority and attorney review.

What information do I need to create a will or trust?

Family, beneficiaries, fiduciaries, assets, debts, existing documents, and concerns are a good starting point.

What asset information should I gather?

Real estate, accounts, retirement, insurance, business interests, vehicles, valuable property, digital assets, debts, and account titling and beneficiary notes.

What beneficiary information should I gather?

Names, relationships, intended shares, specific gifts, backups, and special planning flags.

Who should I choose as executor, trustee, guardian, or agent?

The system helps organize choices and backups. It does not decide who is legally best.

What if I have minor children?

Guardian choices, backup guardians, beneficiary planning, and possible trust questions should be discussed with an attorney.

What if I own a business?

Business interests may require estate, tax/accounting, and succession review.

What if I own real estate in more than one state?

Out-of-state property may require additional planning and state-specific review.

What if someone already passed away?

Start with date of death, state/county, will/no-will facts, court papers, family, assets, debts, and deadlines.

What is probate?

Probate is a court-supervised process in many estates. The details vary by state and county.

What if there is no will?

Administration or other state-specific procedures may apply. Attorney review is often helpful.

What is an executor?

A person named in a will who may be authorized by the court to handle estate administration.

What is an administrator?

A person who may be authorized by the court to handle an estate when there is no will or no executor able to serve.

What are letters testamentary?

Court-issued authority for an executor in many probate matters.

What are letters of administration?

Court-issued authority for an administrator in many no-will or related estate matters.

What is trust administration?

The process of managing trust assets, notices, accounting, and distributions under the trust and applicable law.

What if family members disagree?

Family conflict is an attorney-review flag.

Can I upload court papers?

In this workspace, use document descriptions only. Use document descriptions first and upload copies only when secure upload is available.

Is this legal advice?

No. This site does not provide legal advice.

Are you a law firm?

No. This site is not a law firm.

Does using this site create an attorney-client relationship?

No. An attorney-client relationship begins only if you sign an agreement with a licensed attorney or law firm.

When do I need an attorney?

Attorney review may be important for disputes, deadlines, minors, real estate, business interests, special needs, tax/accounting issues, or probate court papers.

Is the first starting point free?

Yes. The first Estate & Probate Starting Point is free.

Is this available outside New York and New Jersey?

Other states and D.C. are planned for future rollout. Estate Law Aid currently focuses on New York and New Jersey first.

What can I finish before paying?

You can use the free starting path to organize the situation, identify likely next steps, and decide whether a draft, download, review option, or attorney contact is worth pursuing.

Will my draft be complete just because I downloaded it?

No. Downloads are working materials until the correct review and finalization steps are complete.
