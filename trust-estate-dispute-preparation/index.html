Neutral preparation tool

Organize a trust or estate dispute before the next conversation.

Separate dates, documents, people, disputed acts, and open questions so a lawyer can assess the situation more efficiently.

Not legal advice. Deadlines, standing, venue, preservation duties, and available remedies vary by jurisdiction and facts. Do not rely on this page to calculate a deadline.

1. Build a neutral timeline

  • Date of death, incapacity, or triggering event.
  • Dates of wills, codicils, trusts, amendments, appointments, removals, notices, distributions, transfers, and court papers.
  • For each entry, mark whether it is known, disputed, or unknown and identify the supporting document or witness.

2. Preserve and separate documents

  • Wills, codicils, trusts, amendments, accountings, statements, deeds, titles, beneficiary designations, correspondence, notices, and court orders.
  • Preserve originals. Work from copies where practical. Do not write on original estate-planning or court documents.
  • Keep sensitive identifiers and private financial material out of ordinary email or public tools unless a secure accepted channel is confirmed.

3. Map roles and disputed acts

  • Identify the decedent or settlor, fiduciaries, beneficiaries, witnesses, caregivers, advisers, and institutions.
  • Describe the specific act or omission being questioned without assuming motive or wrongdoing.
  • Separate what you personally observed from what someone else reported.

4. Questions to take to counsel

  • What deadlines, notice rules, standing rules, and forum choices apply?
  • What evidence should be preserved now?
  • Are temporary relief, an accounting, information rights, mediation, or other procedures relevant?
  • What are the likely cost, timing, confidentiality, and settlement considerations?

Choose and verify counsel independently.

A directory profile is only a sourced starting point. Confirm current registration, jurisdiction, experience, conflicts, availability, scope, fees, and written engagement terms directly.
